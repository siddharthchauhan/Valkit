Part 11 validation-as-code for LLM agents

1 · Specification

What the agent is for, how it is used, what it must achieve and who signs it off. The risk assessment, the requirements and the test cases are all derived from this — nothing here is entered twice.

2 · Validation run

Runs the acceptance battery, computes each metric as a one-sided lower confidence bound, records the test executions and generates the package. It stops before signing: a pipeline that signed on your behalf would defeat the point of requiring a signature.

3 · Acceptance

The lower bound, not the observed rate, is the claim. The observed rate describes the sample; the bound is what the sample supports.

4 · The validated gate

Every condition is evaluated independently and reported, so you see everything outstanding rather than the first thing.

6 · Electronic signature

21 CFR Part 11 subpart C. Your credential is sent in the request body over the connection and nowhere else: it is never a query parameter, never logged, and never written to the audit trail, which records that a signing happened and which kinds of component satisfied it.

Registering a signer here exists so the console is usable end to end. A real deployment binds the identity store to your directory, and the identity proofing and the 11.100(b) and (c) certification that must precede it are organisational controls no API call can supply.

Integrity

Separate from acceptance throughout. "The evidence cannot be trusted" and "the agent did not meet its target" are different conversations.

ValKit does not make anyone compliant. It produces evidence and documents. It does not determine your context of use, curate your qualification set, replace the critical thinking GAMP 5 requires, or substitute for a quality system.
